About me

Tipu Sultan

Multimodal intelligence for robots that work with people.

I am a Ph.D. researcher in Aerospace & Mechanical Engineering at Saint Louis University, advised by Prof. Madi Babaiasl in the MechaRithm Lab. I combine EEG, EMG, eye tracking, vision-language-action models, and robot learning to build human–robot interaction systems that understand intent and act safely under uncertainty.

My background spans mechatronics and data science. I test my ideas on synchronized human-sensing systems and on a Kinova Gen3 robot arm, and I care most about methods that stay dependable when sensors are noisy, people differ, and task conditions change.

My research

Human signals into reliable robot behavior

My work connects neural and behavioral sensing, uncertainty-aware learning, vision-language-action (VLA) models, and robot decision-making. The goal is not only to predict what a person wants, but to determine whether the evidence is reliable enough—and whether the robot’s next action preserves the person’s ability to change course.

BioGazeFormer · Reliability-discounted evidential fusion

Sensor fault or unclear intent?

When an intent decoder is unsure, two different things can be wrong: a sensor is failing, or the person has not decided yet. The first calls for a sensor check, the second for waiting, and a decoder that reports only its confidence cannot tell them apart.

BioGazeFormer gives each sensor a reliability with a defined meaning, the share of its evidence that survives damage, learns it from a library of sensor faults, and discounts each sensor's evidence by it before fusion. Trained on the moments when people switch from rest into a task, it also flags changing intent. In leave-one-subject-out tests on 22 people, it told a failing sensor from changing intent with an AUROC of 0.90 (0.65 for evidential fusion without supervised reliability) and matched the accuracy of the best fusion baselines; on 8 more people never used in development, its accuracy again matched the best baseline.

  • 0.90 AUROC Failing sensor vs changing intent
  • 87% Damaged sensor named (trained damage types)
  • 8.1 ms Per window on a CPU

T‑RO concept

Proactive assistance under partial intent

An HRI system rarely has a complete specification of what a person wants or what the environment will allow. This work studies how a robot can make useful progress under that uncertainty while preserving human revision, recoverability, and safe commitment.

The central question is when the robot should continue gathering evidence, take a reversible step, or commit to an action. The aim is proactive assistance that remains responsive to the person—not autonomy that gets ahead of them.
